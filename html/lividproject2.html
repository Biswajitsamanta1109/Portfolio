Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting



Piezoelectric energy harvesting converts mechanical vibrations, stress, or movement into usable electrical power. When mechanical force is applied to a piezoelectric material, it deforms the crystalline structure. This deformation shifts the material's internal electrical dipoles, which generates a localized electric field and produces a usable electrical charge and voltage.

Working

The 4-Step Process

  • Mechanical Stress: When we apply mechanical force (like stepping, pressing, or vibrating a surface) to a piezoelectric material like quartz or specialized ceramics, the material gets deformed. Due to the piezoelectric effect, electric charges separate inside the material, generating a voltage across its terminals.
  • Charge Generation: The physical pressure shifts the internal structure of the material, forcing positive and negative ions to separate. This instantly generates a small voltage.
  • Rectification: The electricity generated is in the form of alternating current (AC). A small circuit board with a bridge rectifier is used to convert this into direct current (DC).
  • Storage & Use: Because the electrical pulses are usually tiny and small, the DC power is sent into a capacitor or a rechargeable battery to build up a usable charge.
